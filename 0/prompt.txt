On miTch-Transparency-Layer: yes — that's not just related, it's essentially the same idea we were just discussing, already built in a different form. I looked at the repo: a Manifest V3 Chrome extension that surfaces what websites can technically observe — cookies, storage, fingerprinting surfaces (Canvas/WebGL/AudioContext/WebRTC), permission surfaces — with a three-layer report (observed facts / potential access / unknowns) and the honest core principle "we show what is technically observable, we do not claim what happens on remote servers."

That principle is exactly the guardrail from our discussion, and it suggests how the two projects fit together without either losing its point:

    The extension is per-site, on any page. It's the general "what can this site see" awareness tool.
    The wallet's pre-login insight is per-visit and person-specific ("this is what's out there about you, and we didn't even have to ask"), computed on-device, discarded immediately, never feeding decisions, no server lookups. It's also exactly where miTch's consent flow begins, so it doubles as the demo's opening act.

Rather than building the wallet panel from scratch, the wallet's design note should mine the extension for its taxonomy — the three-layer report format (observed / potential / unknown) is arguably better than my earlier "mark estimates as estimates" phrasing, because it structurally separates fact from inference from unknown. Porting that structure into the wallet's welcome/locked screen would keep the two projects aligned and the language consistent.

Practical suggestion: keep them separate codebases (extension ≠ wallet dependency; the wallet must stay edge-first with zero network lookups), but link them. A short design note in the miTch repo for the pre-login panel, referencing the extension's report structure, tracked as its own issue — and it stays out of the passkey work's way. Then, when PR 2 and PR 3 of the passkey track are done, the panel can be picked up with the taxonomy already settled.
